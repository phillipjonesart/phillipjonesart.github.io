War Around the World

Three battles on three continents, each seen through very different eyes.

The Battle of Trafalgar, 21 October 1805 · J. M. W. Turner, 1822–24

Off the coast of Spain, Admiral Nelson led 27 British ships against 33 French and Spanish ones. Instead of lining up, he sailed straight at the enemy and cut through their line. Before the fighting, he had a signal flown from his flagship, HMS Victory: "England expects that every man will do his duty." Hours later a sniper's bullet killed him, and he told Captain Hardy, "Thank God I have done my duty." Turner painted the scene almost twenty years later, and sailors complained that he had squeezed several moments of the battle into one picture.

The Mongol invasion of Japan, 1274 · the Mongol Invasion Scroll, about 1293

A Japanese samurai named Takezaki Suenaga had this scroll painted to prove how bravely he fought when a Mongol fleet invaded Japan in 1274. He hoped the government would reward him, and he even traveled to Kamakura to ask in person. The scroll shows him charging the Mongol warriors while an iron bomb explodes in the air, one of the first pictures of gunpowder weapons in battle. In 1281, a second Mongol fleet was wrecked by a typhoon, which the Japanese called the "divine wind," or kamikaze.

The Defence of Rorke's Drift, 1879 · Alphonse de Neuville, 1880

On 22 January 1879, about 150 British soldiers defended a small mission station against several thousand Zulu warriors, holding out through the night behind walls of biscuit boxes and sacks of grain. Eleven of them won the Victoria Cross, the most for a single battle. The fight came hours after a Zulu army destroyed a British column at Isandlwana, and Britain had invaded the Zulu kingdom only days earlier, after an ultimatum no king could accept. By July the British had won, and the Zulu kingdom was broken up.
